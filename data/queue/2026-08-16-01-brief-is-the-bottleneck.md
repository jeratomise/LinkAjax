Drafting used to be the expensive part of B2B marketing. A decent first page took an afternoon. A partner-ready kit took a week.

That scarcity has moved.

Most teams can now produce a draft, a variant, and a translation before the meeting starts. The work that still takes judgement is the brief. Who the buyer is. What proof they will accept. What we will not claim. What decision this asset is supposed to cause.

When the brief is thin, AI is very good at making the thinness look finished. That is how a team can feel productive and still ship work that a regional buyer will not act on.

The practical habit is to treat the brief as the production asset. Audience, constraint, proof, risk, and the decision you want. Then let the model fill the page.

The marketer who can specify that work is more valuable than the marketer who can type quickly. Recruiters already know the difference, even when the job description still says "content".
